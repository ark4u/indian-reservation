# Research Brief: The Indian Reservation System

## Context & Scope
- **Question / Goal:** Comprehensive overview of the Indian reservation system (constitutional framework, judicial precedents, current quotas, and contemporary debates) to establish a new research project.
- **Decision Feed:** Policy analysis, academic inquiry, and comparative governance studies.

---

## 1. Constitutional Framework
- **Article 15:** Prohibition of discrimination; clauses (4), (5), and (6) enable the State to make special provisions for the advancement of SCs, STs, SEBCs (OBCs), and EWS (Economically Weaker Sections).
- **Article 16:** Equality of opportunity in public employment; clauses (4), (4A), (4B), and (6) provide for reservation of appointments, reservation in promotion, carry-forward of unfilled vacancies, and EWS employment quotas.
- **Article 335:** Claims of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes to services and posts, balanced with the maintenance of administrative efficiency.
- **Articles 330 & 332:** Reservation of seats for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.

---

## 2. Key Judicial Milestones & Supreme Court Rulings
1. **Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992):**
   - *Ruling:* Upheld the 27% reservation for Other Backward Classes (OBCs).
   - *Limit:* Established the 50% ceiling on total reservations (except in extraordinary circumstances).
   - *Principle:* Introduced the "creamy layer" exclusion for OBCs to filter out economically/socially advanced individuals.
2. **M. Nagaraj v. Union of India (2006):**
   - *Ruling:* Upheld reservations in promotions for SCs and STs, requiring the State to collect quantifiable data on backwardness, inadequacy of representation, and maintenance of administrative efficiency.
3. **Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta (2018):**
   - *Ruling:* Modified Nagaraj by holding that the State need not collect quantifiable data on the backwardness of SCs and STs, but reaffirmed that the "creamy layer" exclusion applies to SCs and STs in promotions.
4. **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022):**
   - *Ruling:* Upheld the 103rd Constitutional Amendment (2019) providing 10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS), affirming that exceeding the 50% reservation limit is constitutionally permissible for economic criteria.

---

## 3. Current Quota Structure (Central Government Norms)
- **Scheduled Castes (SC):** 15%
- **Scheduled Tribes (ST):** 7.5%
- **Other Backward Classes (OBC):** 27%
- **Economically Weaker Sections (EWS):** 10%
- **Total Combined Quotas:** Up to 59.5% (subject to state-specific variations and local enactments).

---

## 4. Contemporary Debates & Controversies
- **50% Ceiling Breach:** Tensions between state-level expansions (e.g., Tamil Nadu at 69%, Bihar's proposed hikes) and the Supreme Court's historical 50% cap.
- **Sub-categorization within SC/ST/OBC:** Debates on whether most marginalized sub-castes within SC/ST and OBC groups receive equitable benefits.
- **Caste Census & Representation:** Demands for comprehensive socio-economic caste censuses to recalibrate quotas based on contemporary demographic data.
- **Merit vs. Social Justice:** Ongoing public discourse balancing efficiency and meritocracy in higher education and elite public services with restorative justice and representation.
